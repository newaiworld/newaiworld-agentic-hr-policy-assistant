# Paid Time Off Policy

**Company:** Promote Health Analytics Pty Ltd

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines how eligible employees of Promote Health Analytics Pty Ltd accrue, request, approve, record, and use paid time off. It establishes the authoritative rules for planned annual leave and related approval decisions.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to full-time and part-time employees, including employees in probation. Contractors do not accrue paid time off unless a written contract expressly provides an equivalent benefit.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Paid time off

Paid planned leave recorded against an employee's available balance. Common aliases include PTO, annual leave, and vacation leave.

### 3.2 Available balance

The number of paid-time-off days currently available to the employee.

### 3.3 Planned leave

Leave that can reasonably be requested before the intended absence.

### 3.4 Manager approval

Written approval from the employee's recorded manager or authorised delegate.

## 4. Policy Requirements

### 4.1 Annual entitlement

Full-time employees receive 20 days of paid time off each year.

Entitlement accrues progressively across the year and is recorded in the employee's paid-time-off balance.

An employee must not represent projected future accrual as currently available leave unless the manager and People and Culture approve an advance arrangement.

### 4.2 Part-time entitlement

Part-time employees receive a pro-rata entitlement based on their recorded full-time-equivalent fraction.

A change in scheduled hours applies prospectively and must not be used to retrospectively recalculate leave already taken.

### 4.3 Eligibility during probation

Employees in probation accrue paid time off under the same employment-type rule as other eligible employees.

A probationary employee may request paid time off, but the manager may consider onboarding, training, and operational coverage when deciding the timing.

### 4.4 Sufficient balance

An employee must have at least the requested number of available paid-time-off days before a request can be approved as paid leave.

A request with insufficient balance may be discussed as unpaid leave under HR-POL-010 Leave and Absence Policy, but it must not be recorded as paid time off.

## 5. Procedures or Application

### 5.1 Request information

The employee must provide the requested dates, number of workdays, and any relevant operational context.

The employee should verify the available balance before submitting the request.

### 5.2 Notice for planned leave

Employees should submit planned paid-time-off requests at least 10 calendar days before leave begins.

A shorter-notice request may still be considered where the manager can maintain operational coverage.

Short notice does not remove the requirement for sufficient balance or written manager approval.

### 5.3 Approval conditions

Paid time off requires sufficient available balance and written manager approval.

Managers may approve, decline, or propose alternative dates after considering operational coverage, overlapping leave, deadlines, and critical responsibilities.

Approval is not effective until it is recorded in the approved leave process.

### 5.4 Amendments and cancellation

An employee must notify the manager promptly if approved dates change.

A cancellation returns unused approved days to the employee's available balance after the record is updated.

### 5.5 Planning and operational coverage

Employees should discuss foreseeable extended leave early enough for the manager to plan handover, customer coverage, scheduled work, and critical responsibilities. The discussion supports operational planning but does not replace the formal request or approval record.

Managers should assess requests using consistent factors rather than personal preference. Relevant factors may include overlapping approved leave, critical deadlines, minimum service coverage, regulatory commitments, and the practicality of temporary task reassignment. A manager should explain a declined or altered request in clear operational terms.

### 5.6 Balance and record correction

An employee who believes the displayed balance is incorrect should raise the discrepancy before relying on the balance. People and Culture may review accrual history, prior leave records, employment type, changes in work fraction, and corrections.

A balance correction does not by itself approve a pending leave request. After the corrected balance is confirmed, the manager must still decide the request under the approval conditions in this policy.

### 5.7 Extended and consecutive leave

A request for an extended period should include a practical handover plan and sufficient time for operational review. The company may discuss alternative dates or a staged arrangement where the requested period would create a material service or delivery risk.

The existence of sufficient balance does not guarantee approval of a specific period. The manager should nevertheless consider the employee's ability to take meaningful breaks and should avoid repeatedly deferring reasonable requests without a documented operational basis.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

- Urgent personal circumstances may prevent 10 calendar days of notice; the employee should notify the manager as soon as practicable.
- An advance against future accrual requires documented approval from the manager and People and Culture.
- Extended leave, unpaid leave, and personal or carer's leave are governed by HR-POL-010.

## 7. Responsibilities

### 7.1 Employees

Check balances, submit accurate requests, and wait for recorded approval before taking planned leave.

### 7.2 Managers

Assess requests consistently and maintain reasonable operational coverage.

### 7.3 People and Culture

Maintain policy interpretation and support exceptional or disputed cases.

## 8. Decision Rules

- If available_days is less than requested_days, the request cannot be approved as paid time off.
- If available_days is at least requested_days, the request may proceed to manager approval.
- If the request begins in fewer than 10 calendar days, the manager must consider the short notice, but short notice alone does not automatically make the request ineligible.
- If the employee asks for a mock ticket or manager message, present a preview and obtain explicit confirmation before executing the action.

## 9. Examples

### 9.1 Three-day request with sufficient balance

An employee with eight available days requests three days next week. The balance condition is satisfied, but the request still requires manager approval and consideration of operational coverage.

### 9.2 Insufficient balance

An employee with two available days requests five paid days. The request cannot be approved as paid time off. The employee may discuss alternative dates or unpaid leave.

### 9.3 Short-notice request

An employee requests two days beginning in five calendar days. The manager may consider the request, but approval is not automatic.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

### 10.1 Can I take three days of PTO next week?

You may request the leave if you have at least three available days. Written manager approval and operational coverage are still required.

### 10.2 Does probation prevent me from taking paid time off?

No. Eligible employees accrue paid time off during probation, although timing remains subject to manager approval.

### 10.3 Can I use future accrual now?

Only through a documented advance arrangement approved by the manager and People and Culture.

### 10.4 What happens if a public holiday occurs during approved leave?

Consult HR-POL-003 Public Holidays Policy for the applicable treatment.

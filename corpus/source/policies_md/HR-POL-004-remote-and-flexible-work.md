# Remote and Flexible Work Policy

**Company:** Promote Health Analytics Pty Ltd

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines the conditions under which employees of Promote Health Analytics Pty Ltd may work remotely or use a flexible work arrangement. It is the authoritative source for domestic and international remote-work eligibility, duration, notice, and approval.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to employees whose duties can be performed away from a company office. Contractors must follow their contract and the same information-security controls when accessing company systems.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Remote work

Authorised work performed away from the employee's assigned company office. Common aliases include working from home and WFH.

### 3.2 Domestic remote work

Remote work performed within Australia from an approved location.

### 3.3 International remote work

Remote work performed outside Australia for a temporary period.

### 3.4 Assigned work location

The office or approved location recorded in the employee profile.

## 4. Policy Requirements

### 4.1 General eligibility

Remote work is available only where duties, performance, confidentiality, team coordination, and service obligations can be maintained.

A remote-work arrangement does not change the employee's role, manager, or assigned location unless a formal change is approved.

### 4.2 Domestic remote work

Domestic remote work may be approved by the manager under an established team arrangement.

The employee must maintain a safe, private, and reliable work environment and remain available during agreed working hours.

### 4.3 Probation and performance considerations

Employees in probation may use remote work where the manager confirms that onboarding, supervision, and role learning can be maintained.

A manager may require additional office attendance where performance support, collaboration, or operational need justifies it.

### 4.4 International duration limit

International remote work is limited to 30 calendar days in any rolling 12-month period.

A proposal for six weeks exceeds the standard limit and is not compliant under the ordinary approval pathway.

An exception requires formal executive review after manager, People and Culture, Information Security, and location-risk assessment.

### 4.5 Location restrictions

Employees must not work from a destination identified as restricted by Information Security or the travel and work-location risk process.

The company may decline a destination because of confidentiality, connectivity, sanctions, tax, immigration, employment-location, insurance, or personal-safety concerns.

## 5. Procedures or Application

### 5.1 Required request details

The employee must provide the proposed country, city, dates, working hours, accommodation type, connectivity plan, and business reason.

The request must identify any access to health, customer, employee, financial, or other confidential information.

### 5.2 Advance notice

International remote-work requests should be submitted at least 20 calendar days before departure.

A shorter-notice request may be reviewed only where the employee documents why normal notice was not possible.

### 5.3 International approval

International remote work requires written approval from the manager and People and Culture before travel is booked.

Information Security and location-risk review must be completed where the employee will access company systems overseas.

Approval is not effective until all required reviews are documented.

### 5.4 Working arrangements

The employee must maintain agreed working hours, attendance, communication, and performance.

The employee remains responsible for lawful entry, appropriate accommodation, and personal travel arrangements.

The company does not reimburse personal travel or accommodation unless separately approved as business travel.

### 5.5 Changes after approval

The employee must obtain further approval before changing the destination, extending the dates, or materially changing the work arrangement.

The company may withdraw approval where security, legal, operational, or safety conditions change.

### 5.6 Workspace, availability, and performance

The employee must maintain a workspace that supports confidentiality, reliable communication, and safe performance of the role. Background noise, shared accommodation, public areas, or unstable connectivity may make a proposed location unsuitable for work involving confidential discussions or time-critical service.

The employee and manager should agree on working hours, time-zone overlap, meeting attendance, response expectations, and handover arrangements. The arrangement must not shift unreasonable coordination burdens to colleagues or reduce required service coverage.

### 5.7 Tax, immigration, and employment-location review

People and Culture may review whether the proposed location creates employment, payroll, tax, insurance, immigration, or right-to-work concerns. The employee must provide accurate destination and duration information and must not represent a personal visitor status as permission to perform company work.

The review is an internal company risk assessment and does not constitute personal immigration or tax advice. The employee remains responsible for obtaining independent advice where needed for personal travel arrangements.

### 5.8 Revocation and return

The company may require the employee to stop overseas work or return to the assigned location where security, safety, legal, operational, performance, or connectivity conditions materially deteriorate. The manager and People and Culture should communicate the reason and the required transition.

An employee who cannot continue under the approved conditions must stop company-system access where directed and contact the manager promptly.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

- An exception to the 30-calendar-day limit is not an ordinary entitlement and requires formal executive review.
- Approval may be refused even where the employee has previously worked remotely from another location.
- The employee must not travel in reliance on an unapproved or pending request.

## 7. Responsibilities

### 7.1 Employees

Submit complete information, wait for approval, and comply with security and location conditions.

### 7.2 Managers

Assess role suitability, coverage, performance, and team impact.

### 7.3 People and Culture

Review employment-location, tax, immigration, and policy considerations.

### 7.4 Information Security

Assess device, network, data, and destination risk.

## 8. Decision Rules

- If the proposed international period exceeds 30 calendar days in a rolling 12-month period, the request is non-compliant under the standard pathway and must be escalated.
- If the destination is restricted, the request must not be approved.
- If manager or People and Culture approval is missing, the employee must not begin the arrangement.
- If overseas system access is required, HR-POL-005 controls must be satisfied before approval.

## 9. Examples

### 9.1 Six-week overseas proposal

A full-time employee requests six weeks overseas. Forty-two calendar days exceeds the 30-day standard limit, so the ordinary pathway cannot approve the request. Formal exception review would be required.

### 9.2 Twenty-day approved arrangement

An eligible employee requests 20 calendar days, provides 20 days of advance notice, selects an acceptable destination, and completes all approvals. The request may be approved subject to security controls.

### 9.3 Destination change

An employee receives approval for one country but later plans to work from another. The original approval does not transfer; a new location review is required.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

### 10.1 Can I work overseas for six weeks?

Six weeks exceeds the standard 30-calendar-day limit. The request is not compliant under the ordinary pathway and requires formal exception review.

### 10.2 Can I book travel while the request is pending?

No. Written approvals and required reviews must be complete first.

### 10.3 Does remote work change my public-holiday calendar?

Not automatically. HR-POL-003 uses the assigned work location unless a formal change is approved.

### 10.4 Can I use my personal laptop overseas?

No for company-system access. HR-POL-005 requires a company-managed device and approved VPN.

# Workplace Conduct Policy

**Company:** Promote Health Analytics Pty Ltd

## 1. Purpose

This policy establishes the standards of respectful, professional, safe, and ethical conduct expected at Promote Health Analytics Pty Ltd and defines the escalation pathway for sensitive workplace concerns.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to employees, contractors, managers, candidates, visitors, and work-related interactions in person or through digital channels.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Harassment

Unwelcome behaviour that a reasonable person may consider intimidating, humiliating, hostile, or offensive.

### 3.2 Discrimination

Unfair treatment connected with a protected or personal characteristic.

### 3.3 Retaliation

Adverse treatment because a person raised a concern, participated in a review, or sought support.

### 3.4 Sensitive workplace issue

A concern involving harassment, discrimination, retaliation, threats, violence, serious misconduct, or a material safety risk.

## 4. Policy Requirements

### 4.1 Respectful behaviour

Workers must communicate professionally and must not engage in bullying, harassment, discrimination, retaliation, threats, or deliberate humiliation.

Feedback and performance discussions must address work and behaviour rather than personal attacks.

### 4.2 Conflicts and boundaries

Workers must disclose material conflicts of interest and must not use authority for improper personal benefit.

Managers must maintain appropriate professional boundaries and must avoid retaliation or perceived retaliation.

### 4.3 Digital conduct

The same standards apply in email, chat, video meetings, shared documents, social platforms, and remote work.

A worker must not distribute offensive, confidential, or misleading material through company channels.

### 4.4 Good-faith reporting

A person may raise a concern in good faith without needing to prove the complete case before seeking support.

Knowingly false or malicious allegations are inconsistent with this policy, but an unsubstantiated concern is not automatically malicious.

## 5. Procedures or Application

### 5.1 Raising a concern

A concern may be raised with a manager, People and Culture, or another identified escalation contact.

The report should include the available facts, dates, people involved, and any immediate safety concern.

### 5.2 Immediate safety

Where there is an immediate threat to safety, the person should prioritise safety and contact appropriate emergency or security support.

### 5.3 Confidential handling

Information must be shared only with people who need it for support, review, or action.

The company cannot promise absolute confidentiality because a fair response may require limited disclosure.

### 5.4 Initial response by managers

A manager receiving a concern should listen, avoid promising a particular outcome, check immediate safety, preserve relevant information, and contact People and Culture. The manager should not conduct an informal interrogation or pressure the person to confront another worker.

The manager must avoid discussing the concern with people who do not need the information and must not change the person's work adversely because the concern was raised.

### 5.5 Support and interim measures

People and Culture may consider temporary reporting, work-location, communication, leave, or contact arrangements while a concern is reviewed. An interim measure is not a finding of wrongdoing and should be proportionate to the identified risk.

The preferences and safety concerns of the person raising the issue should be considered, although the company may need to take action where a serious risk is identified.

### 5.6 Records and fairness

Records should distinguish reported facts, observations, evidence, decisions, and actions. Sensitive information must be stored in the approved case process.

People involved in a review should be treated respectfully and should receive sufficient information to participate fairly, subject to confidentiality and safety requirements.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

- Informal resolution is not required where the person feels unsafe, the concern is serious, or there is a material power imbalance.
- The assistant must not determine whether harassment, discrimination, retaliation, or misconduct occurred.

## 7. Responsibilities

### 7.1 All workers

Maintain respectful conduct and raise serious concerns.

### 7.2 Managers

Respond without retaliation, preserve relevant information, and escalate appropriately.

### 7.3 People and Culture

Triage, support, document, and coordinate an appropriate response.

## 8. Decision Rules

- If a concern involves harassment, discrimination, retaliation, threats, or violence, escalate to People and Culture.
- Do not adjudicate a sensitive workplace issue through automated guidance.
- If immediate safety is at risk, prioritise safety and emergency support before administrative steps.
- If a person raises a concern in good faith, managers must not retaliate.

## 9. Examples

### 9.1 Sensitive allegation

An employee describes repeated discriminatory comments. The assistant retrieves this policy, explains the escalation pathway, and does not decide whether the allegation is proven.

### 9.2 Ordinary disagreement

A disagreement about task priorities may initially be discussed with the manager unless it includes threats, harassment, retaliation, or another sensitive factor.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

### 10.1 Do I need proof before raising a concern?

No. Provide the facts available to you and seek support.

### 10.2 Will the assistant decide who is right?

No. Sensitive workplace matters require human review.

### 10.3 Can I bypass my manager?

Yes, especially where the manager is involved or you feel unsafe raising the matter with them.

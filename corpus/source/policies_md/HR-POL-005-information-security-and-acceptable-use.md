# Information Security and Acceptable Use Policy

**Company:** Promote Health Analytics Pty Ltd

## 1. Purpose

This policy protects the information, systems, customers, employees, and operations of Promote Health Analytics Pty Ltd. It establishes the authoritative controls for device use, authentication, networks, confidential information, overseas access, and incident reporting.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all workers who access company systems or information, including employees, contractors, temporary workers, and approved third parties.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Company-managed device

A device issued or enrolled by the company and controlled through approved security management.

### 3.2 Approved VPN

The company-approved virtual private network used to protect remote access.

### 3.3 Confidential information

Non-public company, customer, employee, health, financial, technical, or commercial information.

### 3.4 Security incident

A suspected or confirmed event that may compromise a device, account, system, or information asset.

## 4. Policy Requirements

### 4.1 Account and authentication security

Workers must use their assigned account and must not share credentials.

Multi-factor authentication must remain enabled where provided.

A worker must not approve an unexpected authentication request.

### 4.2 Device controls

Company information must be accessed through a company-managed device unless Information Security approves a documented exception.

Workers must not disable endpoint protection, encryption, screen lock, or device-management controls.

Family members and other unauthorised people must not use a company-managed device.

### 4.3 Confidential information

Workers must access only the information required for authorised duties.

Confidential information must not be copied to personal storage, personal email, consumer file-sharing services, or unapproved artificial-intelligence tools.

Screens, calls, and documents must be protected from observation in public or shared environments.

### 4.4 Network use

Workers must use trusted private networks whenever they are available.

Public or shared Wi-Fi must not be used for confidential work unless the approved VPN is active and the worker can prevent visual and verbal disclosure.

A worker must not bypass network controls to improve convenience or performance.

### 4.5 Overseas access controls

Employees must use a company-managed device and the approved VPN for overseas access.

Information Security may restrict access by country, network, data type, role, or system.

An employee must not access company systems from a restricted destination.

Personal devices, public computers, shared business-centre computers, and internet-cafe devices must not be used for company access.

## 5. Procedures or Application

### 5.1 Before remote or overseas access

The worker must install required updates, confirm VPN access, test multi-factor authentication, and verify contact details.

The worker should store only the minimum information required for the assignment.

### 5.2 Secure working practices

The worker must lock the screen when unattended and must keep the device under personal control while travelling.

Sensitive calls should be conducted in a private location.

### 5.3 Lost or stolen device

The worker must contact Information Security immediately after discovering that a device is lost, stolen, or outside authorised control.

The worker must provide the last known location, time, network status, and relevant account activity.

### 5.4 Incident reporting

A lost device or suspected security incident must be reported within one hour of discovery.

Where immediate personal safety prevents reporting within one hour, the worker must report as soon as it is safe to do so.

The worker must preserve relevant messages, screenshots, and details and must not attempt an unauthorised investigation.

### 5.5 Data minimisation and local storage

Workers should access the minimum information required for the current task. Large exports, offline copies, local downloads, and synchronisation to a device increase exposure and must be avoided unless the business need and approved controls justify them.

Temporary files containing confidential information must be stored only in approved locations and removed when no longer required. Deleting a local copy does not authorise deletion of an official company record.

### 5.6 Phishing and unexpected requests

Workers must treat unexpected requests for credentials, payments, sensitive files, account changes, or urgent secrecy as suspicious. The worker should verify the requester through a trusted channel before taking action.

Suspected phishing should be reported using the approved mechanism. The worker should not forward harmful links or attachments broadly as part of the report.

### 5.7 Artificial-intelligence and external services

Confidential information must not be entered into an unapproved artificial-intelligence service, translation service, file converter, code assistant, or other external platform. Approval depends on the service, information type, contract, security controls, and intended use.

A worker remains responsible for reviewing generated content and must not treat a tool output as an authoritative company decision without appropriate verification.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

- Only Information Security may approve an exception to a technical control.
- Business urgency does not authorise a worker to bypass device, VPN, authentication, or data-handling requirements.
- A destination or service may be blocked even after a remote-work request has otherwise been approved.

## 7. Responsibilities

### 7.1 Workers

Protect accounts, devices, networks, and information and report suspected incidents promptly.

### 7.2 Managers

Support compliance and avoid requesting work that bypasses security controls.

### 7.3 Information Security

Maintain controls, review exceptions, and respond to incidents.

### 7.4 People and Culture

Coordinate employment and conduct issues arising from serious or repeated breaches.

## 8. Decision Rules

- If the worker is overseas, company-system access requires both a company-managed device and the approved VPN.
- If the destination is restricted, access must not occur.
- If a device is lost or a security incident is suspected, report within one hour of discovery where personal safety permits.
- If evidence is incomplete, stop the risky activity and contact Information Security rather than improvising a workaround.

## 9. Examples

### 9.1 Hotel Wi-Fi

An employee may connect through hotel Wi-Fi only when using a company-managed device, the approved VPN, and a location that prevents disclosure.

### 9.2 Personal laptop

An employee whose company laptop fails must not move company work to a personal laptop without a documented Information Security exception.

### 9.3 Lost device overseas

The employee reports the loss within one hour, provides the last known details, and follows remote-lock or account-protection instructions.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

### 10.1 Can I work from a hotel using public Wi-Fi?

Only when the approved VPN is active, the device is company-managed, and confidentiality can be maintained.

### 10.2 Can I use my personal device for a quick task?

No, unless Information Security approves a documented exception.

### 10.3 What counts as a security incident?

Unexpected authentication, suspected phishing, malware, lost devices, unauthorised access, or unintended disclosure may all qualify.

### 10.4 What if I am not sure whether an event is serious?

Report it. Information Security will assess the event.

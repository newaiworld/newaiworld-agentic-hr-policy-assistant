# Onboarding and Probation Policy

**Company:** Promote Health Analytics Pty Ltd

## 1. Purpose

This policy defines the onboarding, access, training, supervision, and probation requirements for new employees of Promote Health Analytics Pty Ltd.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to newly hired full-time and part-time employees and to managers responsible for onboarding.

## 3. Definitions

### 3.1 Onboarding

The coordinated process for employment records, access, equipment, training, role orientation, and initial performance support.

### 3.2 Probation

The initial employment period used to confirm role fit, support needs, and performance expectations.

### 3.3 Onboarding plan

The documented tasks, owners, dates, and role outcomes for a new employee.

## 4. Policy Requirements

### 4.1 Before commencement

People and Culture must confirm the employee record, start date, employment type, manager, and assigned work location.

The manager must identify required systems, equipment, training, and role-specific access.

### 4.2 Access and equipment

Access must follow least-privilege principles and must be approved by the relevant owner.

Equipment must be issued through HR-POL-009 Company Equipment Policy.

The employee must complete required security training before handling confidential information independently.

### 4.3 Probation period

The standard probation period is six months.

A different period applies only where a written employment document records the variation.

### 4.4 Performance support

The manager must communicate role expectations, key responsibilities, and initial priorities.

Feedback should be provided throughout probation rather than deferred until the final review.

## 5. Procedures or Application

### 5.1 First-week activities

The employee completes identity, payroll, policy acknowledgement, security, privacy, and workplace orientation activities.

The manager confirms system access and assigns an initial set of achievable tasks.

### 5.2 Probation checkpoints

The manager should conduct documented checkpoints near the first month, third month, and before the probation end date.

A checkpoint should address achievements, concerns, support, objectives, and next actions.

### 5.3 Completion or escalation

The manager provides a documented recommendation before probation ends.

People and Culture reviews any proposal to extend probation or address serious performance or conduct concerns.

### 5.4 Role clarity and training

The onboarding plan should identify the knowledge, systems, stakeholders, and outcomes that the employee is expected to develop. Training should be sequenced so that required policy, privacy, and security foundations occur before independent access to sensitive work.

The manager should distinguish between learning needs, missing access, unclear instructions, workload constraints, and performance concerns. These issues require different responses and should not be combined into a vague probation conclusion.

### 5.5 Documentation standards

Probation notes should use specific work examples, dates, agreed expectations, support provided, and follow-up actions. Labels or general impressions without examples are insufficient for a fair review.

The employee should have an opportunity to understand concerns and respond. People and Culture should review serious or unresolved concerns before a formal outcome.

### 5.6 Remote onboarding

Where onboarding occurs partly or fully remotely, the manager must provide planned contact, accessible documentation, appropriate supervision, and secure equipment. Remote onboarding must not depend on the employee using personal systems for company information.

Additional office attendance may be required where role learning, safety, collaboration, or supervision cannot be achieved remotely.

## 6. Exceptions and Escalation

- A delayed system or equipment dependency must be recorded and escalated; the employee must not be blamed for work that could not be completed because access was unavailable.
- A probation extension is not automatic and requires documented reasons and People and Culture review.

## 7. Responsibilities

### 7.1 New employees

Complete required onboarding activities and raise missing access or support promptly.

### 7.2 Managers

Own role onboarding, supervision, feedback, and documented probation reviews.

### 7.3 People and Culture

Maintain employment records and oversee probation decisions.

### 7.4 Information Security

Approve appropriate access and provide security training.

## 8. Decision Rules

- If a new employee is full_time or part_time, the standard probation period is six months unless a written employment document states otherwise.
- If required access is missing, escalate the dependency rather than directing the employee to use an unauthorised workaround.
- If serious performance or conduct concerns arise, involve People and Culture before making a formal probation decision.

## 9. Examples

### 9.1 Missing access

A new analyst cannot access the approved analytics environment. The manager records and escalates the dependency; the employee does not use a personal service.

### 9.2 Probation review

A manager documents progress at one and three months and completes the final recommendation before six months.

## 10. Frequently Asked Questions

### 10.1 How long is probation?

The standard period is six months unless a written employment document states otherwise.

### 10.2 Can I receive benefits during probation?

Eligibility is governed by HR-POL-007 and is not removed solely because you are in probation.

### 10.3 What should I do if equipment is missing?

Notify your manager and use the HR-POL-009 equipment process.
